## The idea

"Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us." This project is a tribute to Carl Sagan's reflection on our place in the universe. I wanted a page that makes you stop for a moment, moving from a normal portfolio view to a look at our shared home from far away.

## How it works

### 1. Earth's layers
The globe is built from several layers:
- **Surface**: 10k diffuse, bump, and specular maps for terrain and ocean reflections.
- **Night lights**: a separate mesh with an additive material that shows city lights on the dark side of the planet.
- **Clouds**: a floating sphere with 8k textures that drifts independently of the surface, which gives parallax depth.
- **Atmosphere**: a custom GLSL Fresnel shader that simulates atmospheric scattering (the blue rim glow) depending on the camera angle.

### 2. Starfield and shaders
The background is a procedurally generated starfield of 15,000 points.
- Stars are colored by real stellar temperatures: O-type blue, G-type yellow, M-type red.
- Twinkling runs on the GPU. Instead of updating each point on the CPU, a vertex shader uses each star's coordinates as a seed for a sine-wave opacity oscillation.
- To avoid square pixels, a fragment shader draws a soft radial gradient with a power-function falloff for each star.

### 3. Scroll-driven steps with GSAP
The experience is a series of steps. I wrote a custom scroll engine that:
- intercepts `wheel` and `touch` events to trigger discrete transitions,
- uses GSAP to animate the `earthGroup` rotation, its position, and the `PerspectiveCamera` zoom at the same time,
- locks input during an animation so the sequence can't be broken.

### 4. Sound and text
In the outro, ambient music fades in. A custom Framer Motion typewriter reveals Sagan's words slowly, in sync with the camera drifting into the void.

## Built with

- Three.js and WebGL for 3D rendering and scene management.
- GLSL shaders for the atmosphere and the starfield.
- GSAP for the multi-variable animations.
- Framer Motion for UI transitions and the text animation.
- AgX tone mapping for filmic color and high-dynamic-range light.

## What I learned

The hardest part was the perspective transition. Moving the camera from a close-up of one city to a distance where Earth is a single pixel meant tuning the camera's `near` and `far` planes and its field of view. Keeping it smooth on mobile with 10k textures and thousands of shader-driven particles taught me a lot about GPU memory and texture anisotropy.
